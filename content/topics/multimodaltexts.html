Multimodal Texts

Understanding How Different Modes Create Meaning — text, image, sound, gesture and layout working together.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

  • define a multimodal text
  • identify the five commonly used modes of communication
  • distinguish between the modes
  • recognise multimodal texts in everyday life
  • explain how different modes work together
  • analyse a simple multimodal text
  • explain why multimodal literacy matters

What Is a Multimodal Text?

A multimodal text is a text or form of communication that uses more than one mode to create meaning.

A traditional text may rely mainly on written language. A multimodal text can combine written or spoken words with images, sound, movement and layout.

The key idea: the modes are not simply placed next to each other. Together, they contribute to the overall meaning of the text.

A Simple Comparison

TypeExampleMain mode(s)
Mainly a single mode A plain written paragraph Linguistic
Multimodal A website article with a headline, photographs, video and page layout Linguistic, visual, aural, spatial

Note: this is a teaching simplification. Even a printed paragraph has some visual and spatial features, such as font and spacing. The useful question is which modes are doing the main work.

The Five Modes of Communication

A commonly used framework identifies five modes of communication. Different approaches to multimodality may group or name the modes differently, so treat this as a practical tool rather than the only possible classification.

Linguistic Dilsel Meaning created through written or spoken language.
Examples: vocabulary, grammar, sentences, paragraphs, spoken language, word choice.
Simple example: a newspaper headline.
Ask: What words are used? How does the language affect the message?
Visual Görsel Meaning created through things we can see.
Examples: photographs, illustrations, symbols, colour, typography, font size, graphics, video imagery.
Simple example: a red warning symbol.
Ask: What do the colours and images suggest?
Aural İşitsel Meaning created through sound.
Examples: voice, tone, music, sound effects, ambient noise, silence.
Simple example: suspenseful music in a film.
Ask: How does the sound change the mood?
Gestural Jest/Hareket Meaning created through movement and physical expression.
Examples: facial expressions, hand gestures, body language, posture, movement, interaction between people.
Simple example: someone smiling while speaking.
Ask: What does the person's expression communicate?
Spatial Mekânsal Meaning created through arrangement and position.
Examples: layout, spacing, proximity, position, organisation, distance between objects.
Simple example: a large headline placed above a smaller image.
Ask: Where does your eye look first, and why?

Modes Work Together

The most important feature of a multimodal text is not simply that several modes are present. The modes work together to build the message.

Think about an online news story:

ModeWhat it contributes
Linguisticthe headline and the article text
Visualphotographs and graphics
Auralvideo narration or sound
Spatialthe page layout

A dramatic photograph can make a headline feel more urgent. A large, prominent layout can suggest that a story is important. The reader's understanding comes from the combination, not from the words alone.

A very small example: a STOP sign.
Its meaning comes from the word STOP (linguistic), the red colour and octagonal shape (visual), and its physical position at the roadside (spatial). Remove any one of them and the message becomes weaker or unclear.

Example: A Social Media Post

Imagine a short video post with a caption, a photograph, background music, a person speaking, and text arranged over the image.

ModeIn this post
Linguisticthe caption
Visualthe image and its colours
Auralthe background music
Gesturalthe speaker's facial expression
Spatialthe placement of text and image

All of these modes contribute to how the audience interprets the post.

Why Do Multimodal Texts Matter?

Most modern communication is multimodal: websites, social media, video, educational apps, online news and digital presentations all combine several modes at once.

This changes what it means to be literate. Modern literacy is not only about decoding written words. It also involves understanding images, sound, layout and movement — and how they work together with language.

What Is Multimodal Literacy?

Multimodal literacy is the ability to understand, respond to, and create texts that use more than one mode of communication.

Read / interpret
understand images · notice sound · analyse layout · interpret gestures · connect all of these with the language
Create
choose appropriate images · use clear language · organise layout · use sound when appropriate · combine modes purposefully

Multiliteracies

The idea of multiliteracies was introduced by the New London Group in 1996. It expanded traditional ideas of literacy by recognising the growing importance of different communication channels, media and cultural contexts.

In short: reading and writing printed words is still essential, but it is no longer the whole picture.

How to Analyse a Multimodal Text

StepWhat you doQuestion
1Identify the textWhat kind of text is it?
2Identify the modesWhich modes are being used?
3Analyse each modeWhat does each mode communicate?
4Look at relationshipsHow do the modes support or change each other?
5Identify the purposeWhat is the creator trying to communicate?
6Consider the audienceWho is the message for?

Analysis Questions You Can Reuse

  • What words are used?
  • What images are used?
  • What colours are important?
  • What can you hear?
  • What gestures or facial expressions are visible?
  • How are the elements arranged?
  • Which element attracts attention first?
  • How do the modes work together?
  • Who is the audience?
  • What is the purpose?
  • How might the meaning change if one mode were removed?

Quick Example Analysis

Imagine an advertisement showing a runner, bold text saying "KEEP MOVING", energetic music and bright colours.

ModeIn this advertisement
Linguisticthe slogan "KEEP MOVING"
Visualthe runner and the bright colours
Auralthe energetic music
Gesturalthe running movement and body position
Spatialthe large slogan placed prominently

Together, the modes create an energetic and motivational message. No single mode carries it alone.

An Important Distinction

Common mistake: thinking that using many elements automatically makes communication effective.

Better thinking: effective multimodality depends on how the modes work together. An image may be only decoration. The real question is: does it contribute to the meaning?

Key Takeaways

  • A multimodal text uses more than one mode to create meaning.
  • A commonly used framework includes the linguistic, visual, aural, gestural and spatial modes.
  • Different modes contribute different kinds of meaning.
  • Multimodal texts include websites, films, graphic novels, advertisements, infographics and many other digital media.
  • Multimodal literacy means understanding and creating meaning across modes.
  • The most important question is always: how do the modes work together?
